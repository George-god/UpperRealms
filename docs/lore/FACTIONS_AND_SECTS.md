# Factions, Sects and Local Power

**Status:** Established opening relationships and proposed wider political foundations.

## Riverstone Village — Established setting

Riverstone is a village on a mountain road, with lanterns, wary residents and a protective ward. A forest survivor is unusual enough to prompt fear and gossip. Its people depend on practical cooperation: herb preparation, watch duty and the knowledge of elder residents.

The story's first social conflict is **not** a kingdom-wide war. It is the question: *Can a stranger earn trust when their very survival suggests danger?*

### Local roles

- **Elder Shen:** Riverstone elder, authority on local cultivation and a source of measured counsel. “Elder” alone does not establish his formal rank in a sect.
- **Lin Mei:** Herbalist disciple gathering spirit herbs for village purposes. Her specific school, employment, family ties and formal sect rank are not yet defined.
- **Villagers:** Not a faceless crowd; they have legitimate reasons to fear corrupted beasts and the Spirit Veil.

## Messenger of the Azure Gate — Established scene, affiliation open

The morning messenger wears azure trim, asks for the forest survivor and refers to an **outer sect**. The current [story configuration](../../config/story.php) labels them **“Messenger of the Azure Gate” / “Outer Sect Emissary.”**

**Do not silently assume** that “Azure Gate” is the official name of a fully defined sect, a branch of another sect, a place, or an initiation passage. These remain options. The emissary should offer a doorway into sect politics without explaining every power hierarchy in the first scene.

## Sect design direction — Planned systems, not first-arc history

UpperRealms has discussed player/NPC sects with leaders, elders, disciples, ranks or tiers, missions, sect resources, alliances, diplomacy and wars. These are useful constraints when writing quests, but they do not by themselves establish the names of ancient sects or who controlled a given region in history.

Possible tensions worth exploring **after author approval**:
- local communities need the protection of powerful sects, but may resent dependence;
- ambitious disciples compete for scarce manuals, herbs and recognition;
- sect leaders interpret Dao anomalies through their institutional interests;
- alliances can be fragile, with loyalties earned rather than predetermined.

## Character-facing knowledge

At the village stage the player should know that sects exist and cultivation can open paths beyond ordinary life. They should *not* automatically understand sect ranks, treaties, territories, secret techniques or great-power geopolitics.

## Open decisions

1. Is Azure Gate a separate sect, a gateway to a larger faction, or a ceremonial title?
2. Who teaches Lin Mei, and why was she gathering herbs near a forbidden forest?
3. How independent is Riverstone, and who maintains the ward?
4. Is Elder Shen retired, village-appointed, sect-linked or something else?
5. How many distinct sects should the first chapter introduce? Prefer one memorable institution before a large faction roster.

**Writing rule:** Do not assign political alignment such as “righteous” or “demonic” as objective narrative truth based solely on a sect's self-description.
