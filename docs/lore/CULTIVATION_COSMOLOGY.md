# Cultivation and Cosmology

**Status:** Canon Checkpoint 01: approved cosmological foundations; remaining mechanisms and realm nomenclature open.

## The layers of knowledge

1. **Observed:** Qi is tangible to cultivators and manifests in the environment. It can circulate through meridians and be cultivated.
2. **Taught:** Manuals, elders and sect traditions describe how to refine or direct that energy, though they may disagree.
3. **Developer canon:** The Dao's origin and nonlinguistic nature are defined below; characters need not know them.
4. **Unknown:** Whether fate is fixed, what the cracked heavens signify, and the precise natures and motives of individual Outer Gods.

Narrative claims should indicate which layer they occupy.

## Qi and the first cultivation moment — Established scene

Spirit Veil Forest is saturated with luminous energy, but Lin Mei describes its qi as “sick.” A corrupted beast appears nearby. After surviving the fight, the protagonist experiences energy gathering within their body and is identified in the prologue as entering **Qi Condensation**. Lin Mei observes that they did so *without a manual* and is shocked.

The story establishes an *extraordinary occurrence*, not its cause. Do not attribute it to a particular bloodline, reincarnation, hidden immortal inheritance, or special class yet.

**Elder Shen's teaching:** “Cultivation is not power. It is agreement—between breath, heaven, and self.” Treat this as his philosophy until the worldbuilding defines whether this is literally how cultivation works.

## The Dao and tribulation

**Established creative direction:** The Heavenly Dao is central to the setting and ultimately worth protecting. The protagonist hears the dream declaration “The Dao is dying.” The speaker and reliability of this warning are unconfirmed.

**In-game account:** The public Codex calls certain ordeals the **Heavenly Tribunal** and characterizes tribulation as a response to ambition. This may be poetic or theological language.

**Open:** The Dao's exact internal experience and limits, how sects measure its condition, and how individual paths interact with universal laws. Its nonhuman awareness and indirect responses are established below.

## Realms: avoid a silent naming conflict

The prologue explicitly names the first cultivation step **Qi Condensation**. Elsewhere the repository includes legacy realm assets named **Qi Refining**, **Foundation Building**, **Core Formation**, **Nascent Soul** and **Soul Transformation**. The exact authoritative progression names, stage count and gameplay requirements should be verified against live realm data and approved before a unified lore list is published.

Do **not** change realm IDs, migrations, numeric thresholds or the gameplay realm table on the strength of these notes. A possible naming reconciliation is to treat “Qi Refining” as a broad discipline and “Qi Condensation” as a first stage; this is **proposed**, not decided.

## Bloodlines, artifacts and manuals

- **Bloodlines:** Ancestral inheritances and rare awakenings are part of the broader design direction. Their origin and legendary bloodlines are not yet fixed as story canon.
- **Artifacts:** Relics may be equipped, used or awakened/evolved in gameplay design. Their creators and historical provenance require individual approval.
- **Manuals:** Cultivation instruction can be learned, traded or found. The protagonist's exceptional first awakening should **not** make all later learning meaningless.
- **Dao paths:** Differing approaches to cultivation provide identity and philosophical conflict. No path is canonically “correct” by default.

## Plane and scale boundaries

The name **UpperRealms** does not yet fix the number, names or access rules for upper planes. Major ascension details have been intentionally deferred. The world should feel vast without mapping every heaven before the first story arc needs it.

## Writer guardrails

- Never claim every powerful character is aware of the Dao's crisis.
- Avoid declaring that all corruption comes from one cosmic antagonist.
- Keep cultivation and moral choices meaningfully plural.
- Do not have the protagonist effortlessly surpass established experts just because of the opening anomaly.
- Any future metaphysical reveal must reconcile Lin Mei's observations, Elder Shen's teaching and the dream without treating every observation as a perfect explanation.

## Checkpoint 01 — Sanctuary, Silent Heaven and change (Canon)

The Sanctuary is an experimental protected universe created by the First Divine Being. Its laws and protections must sustain life while accommodating renewal. The Heavenly Dao began as a maintenance/adaptation framework and developed nonhuman, nonlinguistic awareness from relationships among universal laws. It is not the creator, Vanguard, or Foundation. It perceives patterns and possibilities; its actions are observable through changes in reality, tribulations and other phenomena. It never speaks, writes literal messages, creates a conversational avatar, or explains its intentions. Even the First Being cannot perfectly command or understand it. The dream voice is not the Dao directly speaking.

Creation Echoes are natural records/disturbances arising from changes in laws; they are part of evolution, not all inherently harmful. Resonance describes successful stable integration of new relationships; Dissonance is dangerous incompatibility or accumulation. Law Scars are damaged or transformed areas associated with severe instability and sometimes forced correction. Suppressing healthy changes may exacerbate instability. Not every magical phenomenon, corruption, illness or creature is Echo-derived.

Primordial Anchors reinforce the Sanctuary and are associated with the Eternal Foundation. Their rigidity can conflict with emerging stable relationships. The First Fracture followed incompatible protective interventions by the two aspects of the First Being; see WORLD_HISTORY.md. Analogies between the Anchors, local formations and living meridians are research hypotheses, not established identical mechanisms.

## Heavenly Titles (Canon)

Heavenly Titles are exceptionally rare manifestations of the Dao, associated with extraordinary achievements, recognition grounded in real influence, and profound resonance with relevant laws. No realm automatically confers them; no reliable numerical threshold, ritual or council can force one. Fame alone is insufficient. Manifestations are nonverbal phenomena; cultivators name and interpret them. The title grants no automatic political authority, moral infallibility, or access to the Dao's thoughts.

- **Title:** Permanent recognition of accomplishments; it is not erased when assistance fades.
- **Heavenly Law Imprint:** A lasting spiritual manifestation that normally persists even when dormant.
- **Heavenly Blessing:** Real assistance enhancing the bearer's existing mastery, with individual expressions. It may be harmonious, fading, dormant or renewed. These are descriptive conditions, not gameplay tiers.
- **Dormancy:** May result from spiritual incompatibility, environmental interference, injury or changes in the bearer. It is not divine punishment. A title holder retains their acquired expertise.
- **Renewal:** In exceptional circumstances a new compatible relationship may allow assistance to return, potentially in a different expression. No guaranteed restoration method exists.

**Divine Healer** is the first developed canon title. It recognizes extraordinary achievements in healing and preservation of life, not simply cultivation power. Its assistance may enhance perception of life-force disturbances, stability of understood treatments or temporary stabilization. It does not grant omniscience, automatic cures or unlimited resurrection. Lin Mei's Grace of Living Harmony and Yun Suyin's Grace of the Quiet Interval are planned future personal manifestations, not starting-era abilities. Other floated titles (Heavenly Forger, Dao Scholar, Eternal Guardian, Celestial Alchemist) remain examples only, not approved systems.
